# Discovery and Evidence

Use this reference when uncertainty is the main design problem.

## Evidence Ladder

Different evidence answers different questions.

### Behavioral product data
Strong for:
- what users do
- where they stop
- frequency
- segment differences

Weak for:
- why behavior occurs
- unmet needs not represented in the product

### Interviews / contextual inquiry
Strong for:
- motivations
- context
- workarounds
- language
- decision criteria

Weak for:
- prevalence
- predicting future behavior from stated preference

### Support / sales / success evidence
Strong for:
- repeated pain
- purchase objections
- operational complexity
- vocabulary

Bias:
- overrepresents users who contact those teams
- sales input may represent buyers rather than end users

### Surveys
Strong for:
- prevalence when sampled well
- segmentation
- lightweight directional signals

Weak for:
- discovering unknown needs
- complex causality

### Competitive review
Strong for:
- conventions
- market expectations
- implementation patterns

Weak for:
- proving a competitor pattern is effective
- understanding this product's users

## Evidence Classification

Always label:
- Evidence
- Inference
- Assumption
- Unknown

Example:
- **Evidence:** 38% of users who start onboarding leave at invite step.
- **Inference:** invite friction may interrupt time-to-value.
- **Assumption:** allowing skip will improve activation.
- **Unknown:** whether invited collaboration is necessary for retained value.

## Research Question Quality

Weak:
"Do users like this dashboard?"

Better:
"Can an operations manager identify the account requiring action and decide
what to do within two minutes?"

Weak:
"Would you use AI summaries?"

Better:
"When reviewing 30 open cases, which information do you need before deciding
which case to investigate?"

## Assumption Mapping

Map assumptions by:
- importance to success
- uncertainty / evidence

Test high-importance, high-uncertainty assumptions first.

Types:
- desirability
- usability
- feasibility
- viability
- trust / safety
- operational

## Lightweight Discovery Plan

1. Decision to make
2. Existing evidence
3. Critical unknown
4. Method
5. Participants / data source
6. Success / failure signal
7. How the result changes the decision

Research without a decision is often theater.

## Synthesis

Do not synthesize into generic personas by default.
Prefer decision-relevant outputs:
- behavioral segments
- jobs / outcomes
- opportunity areas
- moments of friction
- decision criteria
- unmet needs
- current workarounds
- confidence level

## JTBD

Use when it clarifies motivation:

> When [situation],
> I want to [motivation / progress],
> so I can [desired outcome].

Do not relabel every task as a "job". Distinguish:
- goal
- task
- pain
- need
- motivation
- outcome
- job
- feature request

## Discovery Exit

Discovery is sufficient when the team can make the next decision with an
acceptable risk level. It is not necessary to eliminate all uncertainty.
